World Health Organization
Why WHO Guidelines Matter
Sanjeevani's recommendations are directly grounded in WHO public health directives for non-communicable disease prevention.

🏃 Physical Activity

  • Adults should do at least 150–300 mins of moderate aerobic activity per week
  • Muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days per week
  • Reducing sedentary time lowers NAFLD risk significantly
  • Even 30 mins of brisk walking daily reduces liver fat accumulation

🥗 Diet & Nutrition

  • Limit free sugars to less than 10% of total energy intake
  • Reduce saturated fats — replace with unsaturated fats
  • Eat at least 400g of fruits and vegetables per day
  • Limit salt intake to less than 5g per day

🍺 Alcohol Consumption

  • WHO recommends no safe level of alcohol for health
  • Alcohol is a direct hepatotoxin — damages liver cells
  • Even moderate drinking accelerates NAFLD progression
  • Abstinence is the single most impactful liver-protective step

⚖️ Weight Management

  • Overweight (BMI ≥ 25) and obesity (BMI ≥ 30) are primary NAFLD drivers
  • A 5–10% reduction in body weight can reverse early fatty liver
  • Waist circumference is a stronger predictor than BMI alone
  • WHO targets healthy BMI range of 18.5–24.9 for adults
Prevention
Why Early Risk Assessment is Critical
NAFLD is a silent disease — most people have no symptoms until serious damage has occurred.
🔕

No Symptoms Early On

Up to 75% of NAFLD patients experience no symptoms in early stages, making self-assessment the only proactive option.

🔄

Fully Reversible Early

Simple fatty liver (steatosis) is completely reversible with lifestyle changes — but only if caught before it progresses to fibrosis.

📈

Rapid Global Rise

NAFLD prevalence has doubled in the last 20 years. It is now the leading cause of chronic liver disease worldwide.

🫀

Linked to Heart Disease

NAFLD significantly increases risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and chronic kidney disease.

💊

No Approved Drug Yet

There is currently no FDA-approved medication for NAFLD. Lifestyle intervention remains the only proven treatment.

🧬

Progression Risk

20% of NAFLD cases progress to NASH, and 20% of those develop cirrhosis — a life-threatening irreversible condition.

⚠️

The window for intervention is narrow. Once NAFLD progresses to NASH (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) or fibrosis, reversal becomes significantly harder. Knowing your risk today — even without a blood test — gives you the power to act before symptoms appear. Sanjeevani exists to close that gap.
